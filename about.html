About

Why this exists, and what it promises

Singapore has a lot of good writing about faith scattered across it — sermons, denominational reports, the occasional academic paper — and not much that sits in between: careful and grounded, but written so an ordinary churchgoer can actually read it in one sitting. This is an attempt at that middle ground, kept small on purpose.

Scope

What this covers

Faith, church and culture in Singapore, mostly. Sometimes that means working through a dataset or a published report. Sometimes it's closer to sitting with a passage of Scripture, or a cultural moment, and asking what it's actually doing to us.

What it isn't: a news site, a survey of opinions, a comment page on church controversies, or commentary on Singapore politics.

Readers

Who it's written for

Anyone who'd rather think something through than be told what to think about it. That includes pastors, elders and ministry leads making an actual decision, who'd rather make it on evidence than on impressions, anecdote, or whatever the last conference said — the pieces are written to be usable in a meeting, with the argument and the sources both on the page. It also includes anyone in a pew who's been handed a number and would like to know what it actually measures, or who wants to think harder about their own faith than a Sunday usually leaves room for.

Nothing here assumes prior training, theological or statistical. Where a method needs explaining, it's explained.

Corrections

What happens when something here is wrong

Some of it will be. Data pieces here are reconstructions from other people's tables, and a reconstruction can be wrong in ways the original author would spot immediately. Cultural and theological pieces can simply be argued badly, or miss something obvious to a reader who knows more.

The standing commitment: an error of fact or arithmetic is corrected on the page itself, not quietly overwritten, with a dated line saying what changed and what it was before. If a source publishes a correction, the pages relying on it are updated to match, noted the same way. Nothing is deleted to make a past position look better than it was. Disagreement about interpretation is different from an error — where a reading here is genuinely contestable, the page should already say so.

Reporting one

Corrections, sources that don't reconcile, and general feedback can be sent through this form. Anything specific enough to check will be checked.

Method

How the figures here are produced

Figures are recomputed from primary tables rather than copied from a secondary source, and where a published total and a published chart imply a figure that neither states outright, the arithmetic recovering it is shown in full on the page. Derived figures are given exactly where the underlying totals permit exactness, and as a labelled range where they don't. Rounding in a source is treated as rounding, not as precision.

AI tools are used in putting this site together. It doesn't change what any argument here stands on: every figure is checkable against tables linked from the page it appears on. Weigh it on that.

The standing sources

  • Singapore Department of StatisticsThe census and the General Household Survey. The primary source for anything about the national picture.
  • State of the Church in Singapore — Annual SurveyCross-institutional research since 2022 — BGST, Singapore Bible College, Trinity Theological College and Salt&Light. The 2026 edition covers 122 churches and 106,721 regular attendees.
  • Guna Raman — "The decline may be mercy"Salt&Light, 8 July 2026. The pastoral reading of the same census data these pages work from.
  • The Generations Project — Ho & Tan, Graceworks (2023)131 interviews and 1,672 survey responses across ten denominations. The fullest local account of how this looks from inside the pews.
  • Institute of Policy Studies, NUS (2025)Independent academic survey work on religious identity and practice in Singapore.

Finally

None of this is the growth

Thinking carefully is worth doing, and it isn't the same thing as being useful to God. The point of any of this is to see the ground clearly before planting in it.

I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.

1 Corinthians 3:6
