Faith, church and culture · Singapore

Faith that only shows up on Sundays isn't the faith the Bible describes.

Most of us didn't choose our faith so much as inherit it, or drift into it, or pick up whatever version was closest to hand. This is a small, unhurried effort to think more carefully — about Scripture, about the church here, and about the culture that quietly shapes what we assume a Christian life is even for.

Two pieces so far, working from Singapore's own data. More as there's something worth saying.

Reading

Two pieces, and they're meant to be read together

One works from the national census, which counts everybody but only asks what people call themselves. The other works from the churches' own survey, which asks far better questions but only of the churches that answered. Neither instrument can see what the other sees — which is most of why, on first glance, they look like they disagree.

Research paper · 2026 What happened to Singapore's young Christians In 2025 the household survey recorded the first fall in Protestant identification in forty years. The headline number isn't the interesting part. Following the same birth cohorts across five official surveys shows the age at which faith stops deepening has moved earlier with every generation — for those born in the 2000s it peaks at 19 and falls. The turn is visible from 2015, five years before anyone was looking. What we care about

Four things, stated plainly rather than assumed

Quick word on the name: it's old, borrowed from what used to be stamped on official government mail to mark it as duty rather than personal business. We've kept the phrase for a different service — not a career or a platform, but the kingdom Jesus talked about. Beyond that, who we are is simple: a few people who care about the church here, about Scripture being taken seriously, and about faith that means more than an hour on a Sunday.

  • Scripture being true, and treated as true — not quietly adjusted each decade to fit whatever is comfortable this year.
  • The church here being worth belonging to — not just a service attended, but a community that actually forms the people inside it.
  • Being able to read our own culture clearly — Singapore's, and the wider one it increasingly shares — well enough to see where it is quietly forming us, not only when it says something we already disagreed with.
  • The gospel meaning something on a Monday — not a memory from Sunday, carried mutely through the week until the next service resets it.

Putting it plainly: we want Christians here — leaders and everyone else — to hold a faith that is thought about rather than assumed, chosen on purpose rather than carried by default, and real enough to survive contact with an ordinary week. Not faith as decoration on an otherwise ordinary Singaporean life, fitted in around a career and a retirement plan. Faith as the thing the rest of it is actually organised around.

We don't think that comes from better programming, or another course. It comes from Christians getting more thoughtful, more deliberate, and more honest about what's actually forming them. That's the whole aim here. Everything else on this site is method.

How this works

A few things that don't change

Not everything here will start from a dataset. Some pieces will be more reflective, some more cultural, some closer to a direct exposition of Scripture. A few commitments hold regardless of the form a piece takes.

  • Grounded in something real A published table, a passage of Scripture, an actual cultural moment — not an opinion that goes looking for evidence afterward.
  • No one is named Neither a congregation nor an individual, where naming one isn't the point. The interest is in patterns, and in what a pattern asks of everybody sitting inside it.
  • Slowly This isn't a news site and doesn't try to be first. Something appears when there's something worth reading twice. Long gaps are the normal condition here, not a sign of abandonment.

We also use AI tools substantially in researching, drafting and producing what's here, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. Partly that's practical. Mostly it's a small, quiet argument of its own: church leadership that treats these tools as belonging to some other, separate world will find that world arriving anyway, on someone else's terms instead of its own. Learning to use them well — and seeing clearly where they can't be trusted — is going to be an ordinary part of ministry within a few years, not a niche technical concern. This is a small, low-stakes place to practise that in the open.

Ahead

Questions on the list

Not a schedule, and not a promise. Some of these won't survive contact with the evidence, and those won't get written.

  • What happens to the cohort that thins out at 19 — do they come back later, and do Singapore's published tables let anyone tell?
  • What do Singapore's other religious communities do differently — in what they measure, reward or expect of their young — that might explain why some aren't seeing the same fall-off?
  • What would a healthy, non-naive posture toward AI in ministry, pastoral care and formation actually look like, for a church that can't simply opt out of tools its own members already use daily?
  • What does the pay and career structure inside Singapore church ministry select for over twenty years, and is it the same thing the pulpit says it wants?
  • Where does an ordinary Singaporean's week actually get formed — school, National Service, work, screens — and how much of that has the local church ever tried to understand, rather than just compete with?
