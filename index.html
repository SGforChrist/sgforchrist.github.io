Faith, church and culture · Singapore

Faith that only shows up on Sundays isn't the faith the Bible describes.

Most of us didn't choose our faith so much as inherit it, or drift into it, or pick up whatever version was closest to hand. This is a small, unhurried effort to think more carefully — about Scripture, about the church here, and about the culture that quietly shapes what we assume a Christian life is even for.

Two pieces so far, working from Singapore's own data. More as there's something worth saying.

What's here

Reading

Two pieces, and they're meant to be read together

One works from the national census, which counts everybody but only asks what people call themselves. The other works from the churches' own survey, which asks far better questions but only of the churches that answered. Neither instrument can see what the other sees — which is most of why, on first glance, they look like they disagree.

Research paper · 2026 What happened to Singapore's young Christians In 2025 the household survey recorded the first fall in Protestant identification in forty years. The headline number isn't the interesting part. Following the same birth cohorts across five official surveys shows the age at which faith stops deepening has moved earlier with every generation — for those born in the 2000s it peaks at 19 and falls. The turn is visible from 2015, five years before anyone was looking. What we care about

Four things, stated plainly rather than assumed

Who we are is simple: a few people who care about the church here, about Scripture being taken seriously, and about faith that means more than an hour on a Sunday.

  • Scripture being true, and treated as true — not quietly adjusted each decade to fit whatever is comfortable this year.
  • The church here being worth belonging to — not just a service attended, but a community that actually forms the people inside it.
  • Being able to read our own culture clearly — Singapore's, and the wider one it increasingly shares — well enough to see where it is quietly forming us, not only when it says something we already disagreed with.
  • The gospel meaning something on a Monday — not a memory from Sunday, carried mutely through the week until the next service resets it.

Putting it plainly: we want Christians here — leaders and everyone else — to hold a faith that is thought about rather than assumed, chosen on purpose rather than carried by default, and real enough to survive contact with an ordinary week. Not faith as decoration on an otherwise ordinary Singaporean life, fitted in around a career and a retirement plan. Faith as the thing the rest of it is actually organised around.

We don't think that comes from better programming, or another course. It comes from Christians getting more thoughtful, more deliberate, and more honest about what's actually forming them. That's the whole aim here.
